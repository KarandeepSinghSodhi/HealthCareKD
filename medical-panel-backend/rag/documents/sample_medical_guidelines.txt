SAMPLE MEDICAL GUIDELINES AND DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA

=== CARDIOVASCULAR DISEASE GUIDELINES ===

1. HYPERTENSION DIAGNOSIS
   - Normal: <120 mmHg systolic and <80 mmHg diastolic
   - Elevated: 120-129 mmHg systolic and <80 mmHg diastolic
   - Stage 1 Hypertension: 130-139 mmHg systolic or 80-89 mmHg diastolic
   - Stage 2 Hypertension: ≥140 mmHg systolic or ≥90 mmHg diastolic
   - Hypertensive Crisis: >180 mmHg systolic and/or >120 mmHg diastolic

2. MYOCARDIAL INFARCTION (MI) DIAGNOSIS
   - Symptoms: Chest pain/pressure, dyspnea, diaphoresis, palpitations
   - Diagnostic criteria: Two of three components:
     a) ECG changes (ST elevation, T-wave inversion)
     b) Elevated cardiac biomarkers (troponin, CK-MB)
     c) Clinical history consistent with MI
   - Risk factors: Smoking, diabetes, hypertension, family history, obesity
   - Treatment: Aspirin, anticoagulation, beta-blockers, ACE inhibitors, statins

3. HEART FAILURE MANAGEMENT
   - Classification: NYHA Stages I-IV based on symptoms and exercise tolerance
   - Systolic dysfunction: LVEF <40%
   - Diastolic dysfunction: LVEF ≥50% with evidence of diastolic impairment
   - Core medications: ACE-I/ARB, beta-blockers, MRAs, SGLT2 inhibitors
   - Monitoring: BNP/NT-proBNP levels, ejection fraction, functional capacity

=== DERMATOLOGIC CONDITIONS ===

1. ACNE VULGARIS
   - Severity assessment: Mild (comedones only), Moderate (papules/pustules), Severe (nodules/cysts)
   - Pathogenesis: Follicular hyperkeratinization, sebaceous gland activity, C. acnes, inflammation
   - First-line treatments: Benzoyl peroxide, retinoids, topical antibiotics
   - Second-line: Oral antibiotics (doxycycline, erythromycin), hormonal therapy
   - Severe acne: Isotretinoin (requires strict monitoring: LFTs, lipids, pregnancy status)

2. MELANOMA SCREENING
   - ABCDE Rule: Asymmetry, Border irregularity, Color variation, Diameter >6mm, Evolution/change
   - Risk factors: UV exposure, family history, fair skin, atypical nevus syndrome
   - Subtype-specific features:
     - Superficial spreading: Most common, variable pigmentation
     - Nodular: Uniform dark color, rapid growth
     - Lentigo maligna: Older patients, sun-exposed areas
   - Prognosis: Based on Breslow thickness, mitotic rate, ulceration status

3. PSORIASIS MANAGEMENT
   - Plaque psoriasis: Well-demarcated erythematous plaques with silvery scale
   - Trigger factors: Infections, stress, certain medications, skin trauma
   - Treatment ladder:
     - Mild: Topical corticosteroids, vitamin D analogs, emollients
     - Moderate: Phototherapy (PUVA, NB-UVB), systemic retinoids
     - Severe: Biologic agents (TNF-alpha inhibitors, IL-17 inhibitors, IL-23 inhibitors)

=== ENDOCRINE CONDITIONS ===

1. DIABETIC SCREENING AND DIAGNOSIS
   - Normal fasting glucose: <100 mg/dL
   - Prediabetes: 100-125 mg/dL fasting
   - Diabetes: ≥126 mg/dL fasting (on two occasions) OR random ≥200 mg/dL with symptoms
   - HbA1c target: <7% for most patients, individualize based on age/comorbidities
   - Screening interval: Every 3 years if normal, annually if prediabetes

2. THYROID DYSFUNCTION
   - Hypothyroidism: TSH elevated, Free T4 low
   - Hyperthyroidism: TSH suppressed, Free T4 elevated
   - TSH targets: 0.5-5.0 mIU/L (normal), goals vary by condition
   - Initial therapy: Levothyroxine for hypothyroidism, PTU/propranolol for hyperthyroidism
   - Monitoring: Repeat TFTs 6-8 weeks after dose change, then annually

3. METABOLIC SYNDROME CRITERIA (IDF)
   - Central obesity (waist circumference) + 2 of:
     a) Elevated triglycerides (≥150 mg/dL)
     b) Reduced HDL (<40 mg/dL men, <50 mg/dL women)
     c) Elevated BP (≥130/85 mmHg)
     d) Elevated fasting glucose (≥100 mg/dL)
   - Management: Weight loss, diet, exercise, pharmacotherapy if indicated

=== NEUROLOGICAL DISORDERS ===

1. MIGRAINE DIAGNOSIS
   - Criteria: 5+ episodes of headache lasting 4-72 hours
   - Features: Unilateral, pulsating, moderate-severe intensity, aggravated by activity
   - Associated symptoms: Nausea/vomiting, photophobia, phonophobia
   - Aura: Reversible focal neurological symptoms preceding headache
   - Preventive therapy: Beta-blockers, TCAs, topiramate, CGRP inhibitors

2. SEIZURE DISORDER MANAGEMENT
   - Seizure types: Generalized (tonic-clonic, absence, myoclonic) vs Focal
   - Diagnosis: Clinical history, EEG findings, neuroimaging
   - First-line AEDs: Levetiracetam, lamotrigine, valproic acid
   - Goals: Seizure freedom, minimize adverse effects, avoid polypharmacy when possible
   - Monitoring: Drug levels if indicated, liver/renal function, blood counts

3. PARKINSON'S DISEASE
   - Cardinal features: Bradykinesia + resting tremor AND/OR rigidity
   - Supporting criteria: Asymmetric onset, excellent levodopa response, no atypical features
   - Early management: Levodopa, dopamine agonists, MAO-B inhibitors
   - Complications: Motor fluctuations, dyskinesias (manage with dose optimization, extended-release formulations)
   - Non-motor symptoms: Depression, anxiety, cognitive decline, autonomic dysfunction

=== ALLERGIC AND IMMUNOLOGIC CONDITIONS ===

1. ALLERGIC RHINITIS
   - Classification: Intermittent vs Persistent; Mild vs Moderate-Severe
   - Common allergens: Pollens, dust mites, pet dander, mold
   - Management: Avoidance, antihistamines, intranasal corticosteroids, decongestants
   - Severe cases: Allergen immunotherapy (3-5 years), monoclonal antibodies

2. ASTHMA SEVERITY AND CONTROL
   - Severity assessment: Intermittent, Mild-Persistent, Moderate-Persistent, Severe-Persistent
   - Control assessment: Well-controlled, Not well-controlled, Very poorly controlled
   - Step-wise therapy: Short-acting β-agonist → ICS → ICS/LABA → Add LTRA/theophylline → Increase doses
   - Triggers: Allergens, infections, exercise, GERD, environmental irritants
   - Monitoring: PEF/FEV1 trending, exacerbation frequency, nocturnal symptoms

3. ANAPHYLAXIS MANAGEMENT
   - Immediate: Epinephrine IM 0.3-0.5mg (1:1000) into lateral thigh, repeat q5-15min
   - Supportive: IV access, antihistamines (H1/H2 blockers), corticosteroids
   - Triggers: Foods, medications, insect stings, latex
   - Prevention: Avoidance, medical alert identification, carry epinephrine auto-injector
   - Observation: Minimum 4-8 hours for biphasic reactions

=== GASTROINTESTINAL DISORDERS ===

1. PEPTIC ULCER DISEASE
   - Most common causes: H. pylori (90%) and NSAIDs (10%)
   - Symptoms: Epigastric pain, nausea, vomiting, bleeding
   - Diagnosis: Endoscopy with biopsy for H. pylori, serology, stool antigen testing
   - Triple therapy: PPI + amoxicillin + clarithromycin × 10-14 days
   - Follow-up: Confirm H. pylori eradication 4+ weeks post-treatment

2. IRRITABLE BOWEL SYNDROME
   - Diagnostic criteria: Recurrent abdominal pain ≥4 days/month × 3 months
   - Subtypes: IBS-D (diarrhea), IBS-C (constipation), IBS-M (mixed), IBS-U (unclassified)
   - First-line: Dietary modification, stress reduction, regular exercise
   - Pharmacotherapy: Fiber/osmotic agents, antispasmodics, loperamide, lubiprostone
   - Exclude: Alarm symptoms (age >50, blood in stool, family history of cancer) warrant colonoscopy

3. INFLAMMATORY BOWEL DISEASE
   - Ulcerative Colitis: Limited to colon and rectum, continuous inflammation
   - Crohn's Disease: Any site GI tract, segmental inflammation, transmural
   - Induction therapy: Mesalamine, corticosteroids, azathioprine/mercaptopurine
   - Maintenance: 5-ASA compounds, immunosuppressants, TNF-alpha inhibitors, JAK inhibitors
   - Monitoring: CRP, fecal calprotectin, endoscopy for mucosal healing assessment

=== INFECTIOUS DISEASES ===

1. BACTERIAL INFECTIONS - ANTIBIOTIC SELECTION
   - Community-acquired pneumonia (CAP): Oral amoxicillin-clavulanate or respiratory FQ
   - Urinary tract infections: TMP-SMX or nitrofurantoin (uncomplicated cystitis)
   - Sinusitis: Observe vs amoxicillin-clavulanate (if severe or persists >10 days)
   - Otitis media: Amoxicillin first-line; amoxicillin-clavulanate if resistant suspected
   - Cellulitis: Cephalexin or cloxacillin (MSSA); adjust for MRSA if risk factors

2. VIRAL HEPATITIS
   - Hepatitis A: Self-limited, supportive care, prevention via vaccination
   - Hepatitis B: HBsAg positive × 6 months = chronic; treatment indicated if HBeAg+ or elevated ALT
   - Hepatitis C: Directly acting antivirals achieve >95% cure; genotype-guided therapy
   - Treatment monitoring: HCV RNA suppression, ALT normalization, fibrosis assessment

3. COVID-19 MANAGEMENT
   - Risk stratification: Mild, Moderate (pneumonia), Severe (hypoxia), Critical (ARDS)
   - Antiviral therapy: Paxlovid, remdesivir (within 5 days symptom onset for severe risk)
   - Immunomodulation: Dexamethasone for moderate-critical disease (reduces mortality)
   - Vaccination: Primary series + boosters for high-risk; updated vaccines seasonally

=== ORTHOPEDIC CONDITIONS ===

1. OSTEOARTHRITIS (OA) MANAGEMENT
   - Pathophysiology: Cartilage degeneration, osteophyte formation, joint space narrowing
   - Risk factors: Age, obesity, joint trauma, repetitive stress, genetic predisposition
   - Staging: Mild (minimal cartilage loss), Moderate (cartilage < 50%), Severe (near bone-on-bone)
   - Treatment: Weight loss, exercise, NSAIDs, intra-articular corticosteroid injections
   - Surgical: Total joint replacement if conservative failed and functional limitation severe

2. RHEUMATOID ARTHRITIS (RA)
   - Diagnostic criteria: Symmetrical joint swelling/pain + positive RF/anti-CCP + elevated inflammatory markers
   - Genetic: HLA-DR4 and HLA-DR1 association (shared epitope)
   - Early intervention: DMARDs within 3 months of symptom onset improves outcomes
   - First-line DMARD: Methotrexate monotherapy or in combination
   - Biologic agents: TNF inhibitors, IL-6 inhibitors, IL-17 inhibitors for DMARD-inadequate response

3. ACUTE INJURY MANAGEMENT (RICE Protocol)
   - Rest: Avoid activities that aggravate injury
   - Ice: 15-20 minutes every 2-3 hours for first 48-72 hours
   - Compression: Elastic bandage to reduce swelling
   - Elevation: Above heart level to minimize edema
   - Pharmacotherapy: Acetaminophen, NSAIDs; muscle relaxants if severe spasm
   - Rehabilitation: Graduated return to activity, physical/occupational therapy as indicated

=== PSYCHIATRIC DISORDERS ===

1. MAJOR DEPRESSIVE DISORDER
   - Criteria: ≥5 symptoms for ≥2 weeks including depressed mood/anhedonia
   - Symptoms: Sleep changes, appetite changes, fatigue, worthlessness, concentration difficulty, suicidal ideation
   - Assessment: Screen with PHQ-9; assess suicidality and psychosis
   - First-line: SSRIs (fluoxetine, sertraline, paroxetine, citalopram, escitalopram)
   - Augmentation: Atypical antipsychotic or buspirone if inadequate response after 4-6 weeks at therapeutic dose

2. ANXIETY DISORDERS
   - Generalized Anxiety: Excessive worry ≥6 months affecting daily function
   - Panic Disorder: Recurrent unexpected panic attacks + anticipatory anxiety
   - Social Anxiety: Fear/anxiety in social situations, avoidance behavior
   - Agoraphobia: Fear of crowds, public transport, open spaces; avoidance
   - Treatment: CBT first-line; SSRIs preferred pharmacotherapy; benzodiazepines short-term only

3. BIPOLAR DISORDER MANAGEMENT
   - Type I: Mania (≥7 days) + major depressive episodes; may alternate or have mixed
   - Type II: Hypomania (≥4 days) + major depressive episodes; depressive episodes more frequent
   - Mood stabilizers: Lithium (narrow therapeutic window 0.6-1.2 mEq/L), valproate, lamotrigine, carbamazepine
   - Antipsychotics: Olanzapine, quetiapine, lurasidone, aripiprazole for acute mania/maintenance
   - Psychosocial: Psychoeducation, family therapy, sleep hygiene critical for relapse prevention

=== PEDIATRIC CONDITIONS ===

1. ACUTE OTITIS MEDIA (AOM)
   - Criteria: Acute onset + otoscopic findings (bulging, opacity, level, perforation/discharge) + signs of inflammation
   - Most common pathogens: S. pneumoniae, H. influenzae, M. catarrhalis
   - Treatment: Observation for 48-72 hours if mild in age >6 months and no severe symptoms
   - Antibiotic: Amoxicillin first-line; amoxicillin-clavulanate if resistant risk
   - Complications: Perforation, mastoiditis, meningitis (rare); analgesia important

2. BRONCHIOLITIS
   - Pathophysiology: Viral (RSV >90%) inflammation of small airways in infants <2 years
   - Presentation: Upper respiratory symptoms → cough, tachypnea, wheezing, crackles
   - Diagnosis: Clinical; rapid RSV testing or multiplex PCR if indicated
   - Management: Supportive care (oxygen, fluids, mechanical ventilation if severe)
   - No routine: Antibiotics, antivirals, or corticosteroids UNLESS evidence of bacterial superinfection

3. CHICKENPOX (VARICELLA) INFECTION AND VACCINATION
   - Viral pathology: VZV; first exposure = chickenpox; reactivation = shingles (decades later)
   - Progression: Prodrome → maculopapular → vesicular ("crops") → pustular → crusting over 7-10 days
   - Complications: Secondary bacterial infection, pneumonia, encephalitis, dissemination in immunocompromised
   - Vaccination: 2 doses age 12 months and 4-6 years; effectiveness >90% for prevention, >95% for severe disease
   - Treatment: Acyclovir if given within 24h of rash onset shortens duration; supportive care for uncomplicated
